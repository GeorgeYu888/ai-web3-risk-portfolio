# Interview Playbook - AI, Web3, Fraud And Risk Operations

## 60-Second Project Explanation

I built this portfolio to show how I approach practical risk and operations analyst work. The data is synthetic, but the workflow is realistic: I take raw events, classify risk signals, create a review queue, write reviewer notes and generate dashboards that a manager could use to see where risk is building. I also show how I would use AI-style workflow thinking carefully, with human review and clear boundaries rather than treating AI output as an automatic decision.

## How I Would Explain The Crypto Risk Dashboard

The dashboard starts with transaction-like events containing amount, asset, country, device, login or payment friction and withdrawal indicators. The Python script assigns explainable points for each signal, such as large transfer value, new device, failed attempt, country change or high-value withdrawal. It then maps those reasons into typologies such as account takeover signal, high-value outflow or authentication/payment friction.

The important part is explainability. A reviewer can see why a case is high priority, not just that the score is high.

## How I Would Explain The AI Ops Workflow Agent

The workflow agent is deliberately simple. It classifies short case descriptions into queues and writes a bounded reviewer note. I would not present this as a production AI model. I would present it as a prototype of the logic a reviewer needs to check: classification, rationale, next action and escalation boundary.

## How I Would Explain The Operations Dashboard

The operations dashboard uses service-style data: issue type, source, turnaround and owner. It is included because my real transferable work comes from operations and customer/process data, not from claiming formal exchange or AML employment. The dashboard shows how I can turn messy everyday data into a management view.

## Likely Interview Questions

### Why should we consider you if you have not held the exact title?

Because the daily mechanics are transferable: review a queue, check evidence, classify issues, document what happened, communicate next steps and escalate when a risk or exception is outside my authority. I am clear that I need to learn the employer's specific policy and regulatory framework, but I can bring disciplined records, SQL/Python/AI tooling and careful customer/process communication from day one.

### Did you use real customer or exchange data?

No. The data is synthetic. I built the project to show workflow thinking and analyst judgement without using private customer, employer, exchange, banking or identity data.

### Is this a machine-learning model?

No. It is intentionally rule-based and explainable. For the roles I am applying for, the goal is not to impress with a black-box model. The goal is to show I can create reviewable logic, clean notes, dashboards and escalation boundaries.

### What would you improve next?

I would add reviewer feedback loops, false-positive tagging, case aging, queue owner assignment, resolution outcomes, more detailed account history and a separate quality-assurance view that checks whether reviewer notes are complete.

### How do you use AI responsibly?

I use AI to draft structure, classify routine cases and improve speed, but I keep the evidence visible. I would not let AI make a final risk, compliance, credit or identity decision. The reviewer must still check the facts, policy and escalation rules.
