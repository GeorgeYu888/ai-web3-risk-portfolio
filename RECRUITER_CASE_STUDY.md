# Recruiter Case Study - AI + Web3 Risk Portfolio

## Why This Exists

This repo was built as proof-of-work for entry-level and early-career AI, data, fraud, crypto risk, and operations analyst roles.

The goal is to show a practical analyst workflow, not to claim professional exchange, broker, or regulated fraud-investigation employment. All crypto market experience represented here is personal market participation and personal research.

## Role Fit

### Crypto Risk Dashboard

Shows how I would turn raw transaction events into a review queue:

- assign explainable risk scores
- flag abnormal account and transaction behaviour
- classify risk typologies
- create case notes for manual review
- write cases to SQLite for repeatable querying
- generate an HTML dashboard for KRI review

This supports roles that mention risk operations, transaction monitoring, fraud review, risk analytics, KYC/AML-adjacent operations, and business-risk reporting.

### AI Ops Workflow Agent

Shows how I use AI-style workflow thinking without hiding the reviewer logic:

- classify cases into operational categories
- produce bounded reviewer notes
- recommend next actions
- keep human review visible

This supports roles that mention AI-assisted operations, workflow automation, business process improvement, and analyst productivity.

### Operations Data Dashboard

Shows transferable evidence from service operations:

- issue mix
- turnaround patterns
- rework and complaint signals
- manager-facing summary output

This supports roles that need data communication, dashboarding, cross-functional follow-up, and practical process control.

## How This Maps To Current Applications

The current application queue includes Binance risk and AI/KYB/KYC roles, PointsBet payments/fraud/identity, DigiCert customer authentication, Airwallex credit risk operations, and AustralianSuper AML/CTF and fraud. These roles differ by industry, but the common daily mechanics are similar:

- review a queue of cases or transactions
- check whether evidence supports escalation
- write clean reviewer notes
- use dashboards or SQL to find patterns
- communicate next steps without over-promising
- keep a clear boundary between AI support and human accountability

The portfolio is deliberately built around those mechanics.

## What I Would Add In A Real Team

In a real risk operations team, I would add case outcomes, false-positive labels, reviewer ownership, SLA aging, quality assurance checks, policy references and escalation reasons. I would also separate model or automation suggestions from final human decisions so auditability is preserved.
